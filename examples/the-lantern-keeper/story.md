# Chapter 1: The Last Light on the Hill

At the top of the hill, where the road gave up and became a path, there was a small stone house with one round window. In the window hung a lantern, and beside the lantern lived Ada.

Every evening, when the sky turned the colour of cold tea, Ada climbed the stool, opened the little glass door and lit the wick. The flame was no bigger than a thumbnail. Still, the boats on the lake could see it, and so could the owls, and so could anyone walking home late with tired feet.

— Why do you do it every night? asked her brother Tomas, who was seven and asked why about most things.

— Because someone might be lost, said Ada.

— What if nobody is?

Ada shrugged. — Then the lantern is just a lantern, and that's fine too.

# Chapter 2: The Night Without Oil

One evening in late autumn, Ada tipped the oil can and nothing came out. Not a drop. The shop in the village was closed, and the village was far.

Tomas looked at the empty lantern, then at the dark window, then at his sister.

— So nobody will see the hill tonight, he said.

Ada thought for a long time. Then she took the old candle from the kitchen drawer, the one they kept for storms, and set it inside the glass.

It burned smaller than the oil flame. It flickered when the wind leaned on the house. But it burned.

Much later, when Tomas was asleep, there was a knock at the door. A woman stood outside with a fishing net over her shoulder and the lake still dripping from her boots.

— I couldn't find the shore, she said. Then I saw your light.

Ada smiled, and put the kettle on.
